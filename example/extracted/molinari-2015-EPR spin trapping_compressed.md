Results of electrochemical studies focusing on application

Photocatalysis, on the other hand, has the important advan-

source of renewable energy for reactions to occur on stable and
environmentally benign semiconductors such as TiO2.
It

bent form, i.e. in a state in which the LUMO is lowered enough
to facilitate electron transfer.7 Conditions become favorable

Reduction of CO2 is a multielectron process that leads to

important contribution. The EPR technique oﬀers indeed a
potent tool for the detection of short-lived radical species.21–24
These considerations are the motivation that led us to
undertake the investigations described in the present paper.
Specifically, we used the EPR spin-trapping technique to
analyse directly the formation and the role of radical inter-
mediates in illuminated deaerated TiO2 aqueous suspensions
containing carbonates, under photocatalytic conditions in
which water oxidation and reduction processes are required to
occur simultaneously.

Experimental
Materials and methods

Several commercial TiO2 samples were used in this study.
Metal nanoparticle modifications of TiO2-P25 were obtained
following the procedure described by Goren et al.15

Apparatus

EPR spin trapping experiments were performed with a Bruker
ER 200MRD spectrometer equipped with a TE 201 resonator,
at a microwave frequency of 9.4 GHz.
Irradiation was
carried out directly inside the EPR cavity with a Hanau
Q-400 medium-pressure Hg lamp using a cut oﬀ filter (λ >
360 nm). The settings for the EPR spectrometer were as
follows: center field, 3460 G; sweep width, 50 G; microwave fre-
quency 9.4 GHz; modulation frequency 100 kHz; power,
6.3 mW; sweep time 41.94 s. The same flat quartz EPR cell was
used for all measurements in order to minimize experimental
